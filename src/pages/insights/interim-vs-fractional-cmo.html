Interim CMO vs fractional CMO: which does your company need?

An interim CMO leads your marketing full time through a specific milestone. A fractional CMO works part time, splitting the week across several companies. If marketing needs to be led — positioning set, a team built, go-to-market rebuilt — you need someone in the seat, and that means interim.

Why the CMO seat is hard to fill part time

The CMO sits at the center of the business. Information flows in from the market, out from product and engineering, and up to the board and investors. Sales needs answers this week, not next Tuesday. When the person connecting all of that is only there part of the time, everyone else waits, and the company slows down.

That's why we believe in interim, not fractional, for the roles that run the business. Fractional works well for functions with clear boundaries and a steady rhythm, such as finance or IT. Marketing at a growing technology company is rarely either.

When a fractional CMO makes sense

Fractional is a good fit when the need is narrow and steady:

  • An early company needs senior marketing judgement, but not yet a leader in the seat.
  • The plan is working and the team is solid; it needs periodic oversight, not change.
  • Budget only allows part-time help, and the stakes of moving slowly are low.

Signs you need an interim CMO

  • You’ve closed funding and have go-to-market goals, but no marketing leader.
  • Your CMO has left, and the pipeline can’t wait for a six-month search.
  • You’ve pivoted, or chosen a new beachhead segment, and positioning, messaging and the team all need to change.
  • Your go-to-market needs rebuilding for how buyers now find vendors, including AI search and chat assistants.
  • You aren’t sure yet what the permanent CMO role should look like.
