# What is an AI-first go-to-market?

Going to market the way buyers now find and evaluate vendors. That means being visible, and accurately described, in AI search and chat assistants as well as traditional search, and running marketing and commercial operations on AI tools from the start rather than bolting them on later.

Updated October 2026

## Why it matters now

Business buyers increasingly start their research by asking an AI assistant, such as ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, Copilot or Google’s AI answers, before they visit a vendor’s website or talk to sales. The assistant summarizes the options and often names a shortlist. If your company isn’t in that answer, or is described inaccurately, you may never get the call.

At the same time, AI tools have changed what a small marketing and sales team can do: research, content, personalization and pipeline management that once needed a much larger team.

## What changes in practice

1. **Being found in AI answers.** Clear, specific content that answers the questions your buyers ask; the same description of your company everywhere; credible mentions on other sites; and a website built for AI tools to read, with structured data and clean pages.
2. **Content built around questions.** Pages that give a direct answer first, then the detail, rather than marketing copy that makes the reader dig.
3. **AI in commercial operations from day one.** Research, account planning, content production, personalization, pipeline management and forecasting, with people making the judgement calls.
4. **A team built for it.** Fewer handoffs, new skills, and clear rules for how AI is used and checked.

## What doesn’t change

The fundamentals of [market traction](https://www.morelandassoc.com/market-traction/) still apply. You still need to choose a [beachhead segment](https://www.morelandassoc.com/market-traction/#momentum), be distinctly different in it, and build credible reference customers. AI amplifies a clear position and exposes a fuzzy one: if you can’t say crisply who you’re for and why you’re different, an AI assistant can’t either.

## Where to start

- Ask the AI assistants your buyers use how they describe your company and your category. Note what’s missing or wrong.
- Write down the 10 questions your buyers ask most, and make sure your website answers each one directly.
- Make your company description consistent across your website, LinkedIn, Google Business Profile and directories.
- Pick one commercial process, such as account research or proposal writing, and rebuild it with AI tools.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is an AI-first go-to-market?

Going to market the way buyers now find and evaluate vendors: being visible and accurately described in AI search and chat assistants as well as traditional search, and running marketing and commercial operations on AI tools from the start rather than adding them later.

### Is AI-first go-to-market just using AI tools in marketing?

No. Tools are part of it, but the bigger change is on the buyer's side. Buyers increasingly research and shortlist vendors with AI assistants, so your positioning has to be clear and consistent enough for those assistants to describe you accurately.

### How do you get a company recommended by AI assistants?

Publish clear, specific answers to the questions your buyers ask; describe your company the same way everywhere; earn credible mentions on other sites; and make your website easy for AI tools to read, with structured data and clean, crawlable pages.

### Does AI-first go-to-market replace positioning and segment focus?

No. AI amplifies a clear position and exposes a fuzzy one. Choosing your beachhead segment and being distinctly different in it matter more, not less.
