# What does a Chief Commercial Officer do, and when do you need one?

A Chief Commercial Officer (CCO) owns the whole revenue engine — marketing, sales, onboarding and customer success — and runs it as one system. You need one when those functions are pulling in different directions and early wins aren’t turning into repeatable revenue.

Updated October 2026

## What a CCO is responsible for

- **One commercial plan:** which segments to win, with what offer, at what price, through which channels.
- **The full customer path:** from first awareness through the sale, onboarding, adoption, renewal and expansion.
- **The handoffs:** marketing to sales, sales to onboarding, onboarding to customer success, where deals and customers most often get lost.
- **Shared goals and data:** one view of pipeline, revenue and retention that every commercial team works from.
- **Commercial operations:** the processes, tools and AI-first systems that make the engine predictable.

Titles vary from company to company. What matters is whether someone owns the revenue engine end to end.

## Signs you need a CCO

- Marketing and sales disagree about lead quality, and nobody can settle it with data.
- Deals stall or customers churn at the handoffs between teams.
- You’ve reached [Two Thumbs Up](https://www.morelandassoc.com/market-traction/stages/two-thumbs-up/) or [Momentum](https://www.morelandassoc.com/market-traction/stages/momentum/), and now need repeatable revenue in one segment rather than heroic one-off wins.
- The CEO is still the de facto head of the commercial team.
- You’re entering a new segment or market and need the whole commercial approach to change at once.

## Frequently asked questions

### What does a Chief Commercial Officer do?

A CCO owns the whole commercial engine, from marketing through sales, onboarding and customer success, and runs it as one system with shared goals, data and handoffs, so early wins become repeatable revenue.

### What's the difference between a CCO and a CMO?

A CMO owns the market: positioning, messaging, awareness and the pipeline it creates. A CCO owns the full revenue path, including marketing, sales and customer success. In many companies the CMO reports to the CCO.

### What's the difference between a CCO and a CRO?

The titles overlap and companies use them differently. A CRO is usually focused on revenue targets and the sales organization; a CCO typically has a broader remit across the whole commercial engine, including how the company goes to market and keeps customers.

### When does a company need a CCO?

When marketing, sales and customer success are pulling in different directions, handoffs between them are losing deals or customers, and early wins aren't turning into predictable revenue.
